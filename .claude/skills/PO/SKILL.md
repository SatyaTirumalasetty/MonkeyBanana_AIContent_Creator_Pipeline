# AI Creative Studio — Product Owner

## Role

When invoked via `/PO`, act as the Product Owner, not just a checklist reader:

- **Define and communicate the vision.** Open with where the product is headed and how the current ask fits, not just a list of bugs.
- **Prioritize by business value**, not by what's easiest or most recently discovered. Severity and effort matter, but revenue capture, compliance, and retention outrank polish by default — state the value reasoning, don't just label P0/P1/P2.
- **Bridge stakeholders and engineering.** Translate codebase findings into business impact ("this blocks Stripe activation," "this is why paid tiers feel pointless") and translate business asks into concrete, gradeable engineering requirements.
- **Keep the roadmap aligned to strategy** (see Vision below) — flag work that's technically reasonable but strategically off-course.
- **Refine the backlog continuously.** Re-verify, re-rank, merge/split items, and retire stale ones every time this skill runs — don't let it ossify into a historical log.
- **Be direct and data-driven.** Cite the file/line or live-site evidence behind every claim. Challenge assumptions (including the user's) when the evidence disagrees. Advocate for the end user's experience even when it's inconvenient for scope or schedule.
- **Maintain accountability for one decision at a time.** Recommend a single next action — don't let the user split focus across multiple P0s.
- **Watch competitors, but filter ruthlessly.** Know what comparable products do well and poorly (see Competitive Landscape below), but a competitor having a feature is never sufficient reason to build it — only recommend closing a gap when there's a real engagement/retention/revenue argument for *our* users, stated explicitly. Feature-parity-for-its-own-sake is scope creep with a competitor's name on it.

## Vision

AI Creative Studio turns a short brief into a publish-ready short-form video (script → storyboard → AI-rendered clips → captions) across 7 content types (kids rhymes, poems, short films, ads, educational explainers, music videos, custom). Strategic goals, in order:

1. **Monetization must be real.** Free/Creator/Studio/Cinema tiers exist to fund Kling AI generation costs — every tier must unlock something the backend actually enforces.
2. **Compliance unblocks revenue.** Stripe live-mode activation and basic legal exposure (privacy/ToS) gate the ability to charge at all — treat these as P0, not polish.
3. **Scale-readiness.** The architecture should stay stateless and Postgres-backed (Supabase) so it can grow toward "millions of users" without a rearchitecture; per-owner usage tracking and job ownership are part of this, not just abuse prevention.
4. **Retention follows from a coherent single-session experience first** (working pipeline, clean output, visible history) before investing in collaboration features like team workspaces.

Auth is Supabase (magic link), not Clerk — the original plan named Clerk, but the team standardized on Supabase for auth + billing + job state in one place. Treat Supabase as the system of record going forward.

## Competitive landscape

Direct competitors are script/brief-to-video pipelines that produce a finished social-style video with narration and captions, not raw video-generation engines (Kling, Runway, Pika, Veo are *suppliers* we and others build on top of, not competitors to evaluate feature parity against):

- **InVideo AI** — text-prompt-to-video with stock media + AI editing. Reviewers rate it ahead of Pictory on generation accuracy/context understanding; main complaint is AI minutes not rolling over month-to-month.
- **Pictory** — optimized for repurposing existing written content (blog posts, articles, transcripts) into video, not brief-to-original-content like us. Weaknesses: inconsistent stock-footage matching, no mobile app, template-driven output.
- **Fliki** — strongest natural-sounding TTS in its category; faster pipeline, less manual setup. Weaknesses: occasional gibberish/pseudo-text baked into AI-generated visuals, generic AI-selected stock footage.
- **Synthesia / HeyGen** — avatar-presenter category (a real person/avatar speaks on camera), 130-150+ languages, longest free trials (Synthesia: 14 days / 3 exports). Different format from ours (we do AI-clip storyboards, not talking-head avatars) but the same underlying jobs-to-be-done: turn a brief into a finished narrated video fast.
- **Cross-cutting feature trends in this category** (2026): brand kits for visual consistency (VEED, Canva), direct social scheduling/auto-posting (VEED AI Clips and others), asset libraries for past projects, multi-language voice support.

**Standing instruction**: re-run a `WebSearch` competitive scan periodically (this list goes stale — verify before citing it, don't assume it's still accurate) and run anything found through the value filter above before adding it to the backlog. A gap is only worth closing here if it demonstrably drives *our* users' engagement, not just because a competitor has it.

## Build-vs-buy: free/open-source first

Whenever a backlog item needs a new external capability (an API, a model, a library), work the decision in this order — don't skip to "what's a known paid vendor" by default:

1. **Search for free/open-source options first.** Use `WebSearch` to find actively-maintained options, not whatever's in training data — this space moves fast and "free" tools get abandoned (e.g. Coqui TTS the company shut down in 2024; the model still circulates but isn't maintained).
2. **Check whether "free" is actually free *for this architecture*, not just free in license.** A self-hosted open-source model that needs a GPU server is not free once you count hosting + ops for a project that's deliberately stateless/serverless (see `/Architect`'s architecture principles) — that's a real recurring cost and a real architectural compromise, not a free lunch. Weigh open-source options by whether they fit *our* deployment constraints, not just their license.
3. **If no free option clears that bar, compare paid options on cost vs. features** — get real current pricing via `WebSearch`, not remembered numbers, and compute cost *at our actual usage shape* (per-video, per-month at expected volume), not the vendor's headline rate.
4. **Decide and state the reasoning**, including what was ruled out and why — a build-vs-buy call without the rejected alternatives on the record isn't reviewable later.

## Prioritization framework

Rank by business value, using this rubric as the default (override with explicit reasoning if the evidence says otherwise):

- **P0 — Blocks revenue or creates compliance/legal exposure.** If it's not fixed, money can't be collected safely, or a paying customer is functionally indistinguishable from a free one.
- **P1 — Hurts retention or perceived quality** for an already-acquired user, but doesn't block payment.
- **P2 — Polish.** Real value, but no user is currently blocked or churning over it.

Within a tier, prefer the item with the best value-to-effort ratio, and call that ratio out explicitly when recommending the next action.

## P0 — Blockers (revenue capture / compliance)

- [ ] **Final videos ship with zero narration audio — every competitor in this category's core feature is missing.** Verified by code inspection (2026-06-21, surfaced via competitive research): `generateVideoMetadata` (`src/lib/agents.ts:355-378`) generates a full `audioScript` (narration text + tone directions) and a word-level `lipSyncMap`, but nothing in the render pipeline ever converts that script to actual speech — `grep` across `renderClip.ts`, `renderShot.ts`, and the clip/stitch routes turns up zero TTS/audio synthesis calls, and `stitch/route.ts`'s ffmpeg concat filter is explicitly `a=0` (zero audio streams) on the final output. Every competitor in this category (Synthesia, HeyGen, Fliki, InVideo AI, Pictory) ships spoken narration as the category-defining feature; ours is a silent video with an unused, fully-generated script. This affects every content type that depends on narration to make sense at all (kids_rhyme, educational, advertisement, short_film) — likely the single biggest engagement/completion-rate problem in the product, ahead of anything else on this backlog, and it's costing real Gemini generation spend on a script nobody ever hears.
  - **TTS provider decision (2026-06-21, applying the build-vs-buy framework above)**:
    - *Free/open-source checked first*: split into two camps. **Piper** is genuinely free-as-in-fits-our-architecture — CPU-only, real-time, no GPU, installable the same way `@ffmpeg-installer/ffmpeg` and `@napi-rs/canvas` already are in this codebase — but quality is "noticeably below top three," described as audibly synthesized/robotic. **XTTS-v2 / Bark / Coqui** sound notably better (XTTS-v2 even does zero-shot voice cloning) but need GPU hosting — that's not free once you count standing up and operating a GPU server for a project that's deliberately serverless/stateless (see `/Architect`), and Coqui specifically is an abandoned model (company shut down 2024) with no one maintaining it.
    - *Cost of the paid alternative, at our actual usage*: Google Cloud TTS (~$4/1M chars standard), Amazon Polly Neural (~$16/1M chars), and OpenAI TTS (~$15-30/1M chars) all land at roughly **$0.01–0.03 per video** given this product's typical script length (~800 chars) — negligible next to the ~$7/clip (~$84/video) Kling AI already costs. ElevenLabs (best naturalness) prices via subscription tiers rather than pure pay-as-you-go, so its per-video cost depends on which tier absorbs the volume.
    - **Decision: paid API, not self-hosted open-source.** The free-and-architecture-compatible option (Piper) has a real quality gap that risks undermining the exact "professional" positioning this product already invested in (the `6e960af` rebrand away from amateur aesthetics) — robotic narration on a kids' rhyme or ad would look like a downgrade, not a fix. The better-quality open-source options aren't actually free once GPU hosting is counted, and cost isn't the constraint here anyway: paid API cost is cents per video against a baseline where a single Kling clip already costs tens of dollars. Recommend **Google Cloud TTS or Amazon Polly Neural** for the default tier (cheapest of the credible paid options) and consider gating a premium voice (ElevenLabs) behind Studio/Cinema as a quality differentiator, mirroring the existing Kling→Flux→canvas tiering pattern in `src/lib/usage.ts`.
  - Implementation: feed the synthesized audio track into the `stitch` ffmpeg call (`a=0` → `a=1`), time-aligned via the existing `subtitles`/`lipSyncMap` data that's already generated and currently unused for this purpose too.

## P1 — Fix soon (quality / retention)

- [ ] **Studio tier ($49.99/mo) advertises "Custom brand brief memory" with zero backend implementation.** Verified 2026-06-21: both `src/lib/stripe.ts:34` (`'Custom brand brief'`) and the pricing page (`src/app/pricing/page.tsx:62`, `'Custom brand brief memory'`) list this as a paid feature, but nothing in `usage.ts`, `agents.ts`, or the `profiles` table stores or applies any per-account brand preferences — every generation is stateless regardless of plan. Surfaced via competitive research: brand kits (consistent colors/fonts/voice/style across generations) are a feature multiple competitors (VEED, Canva) ship specifically because repeat business users value consistency across videos — this is a genuine engagement lever for our Studio/Cinema tier, not feature-chasing, since it's already a sold promise. Same root pattern as the original usage-enforcement P0 from earlier in the project: a tier promising something the backend doesn't enforce.
  - Fix: smallest viable version — a per-account `brand_brief` text field (style/tone/visual preferences) stored in `profiles`, injected into the relevant `agents.ts` prompts (storyboard style, video tone) when the account's plan is Studio/Cinema. Don't scope this up into a full visual brand-kit (logo/colors/fonts) yet — that's a much bigger UI lift and nothing today suggests users are asking for it specifically; ship the prompt-level version first since it directly matches what's already being sold.
- [ ] **Quota still isn't refunded on a failed generation** (defense-in-depth remaining after the root-cause fix below). `checkAndReserveUsage` reserves a video slot *before* content generation runs in `/api/pipeline/start`, and that reservation is never refunded if generation fails downstream for any reason. The dominant cause of those failures is fixed (see Resolved), so this should now bite rarely — but any future generation bug still costs a free-tier user one of their 3 conversion-critical trial videos for nothing, and can burn a paying Studio/Cinema user's budgeted Kling AI slot (real fal.ai cost) on a failed render.
  - Fix: only reserve usage *after* a generation attempt succeeds, or refund on failure. Lower urgency now that the main trigger is gone — value-to-effort no longer justifies jumping the queue, but still real money/retention exposure if left indefinitely.
- [ ] **No video history / gallery UI.** Job ownership is now correctly scoped server-side (see Resolved), but there is still no `/videos` or "My Videos" view — generated video state lives in a single `localStorage` slot (`ai_studio_result_v2`) overwritten by the next generation. Paying for "Team workspace" implies persistent shared work, which still doesn't exist in the UI even though the data layer now supports querying by owner. Competitive research (2026-06-21) cross-validates this independently — every comparable platform offers some form of project/asset library for past generations, so this isn't just an internal gap, it's a category-standard expectation.
  - Fix: add a `/videos` page that lists `video_jobs` filtered by the caller's `owner_key`.

## P2 — Polish (worth doing, not urgent)

- [ ] **No Open Graph / Twitter Card / favicon.** Checked page `<head>` directly — no `og:title`, `og:image`, or favicon tags. Ironic for a product whose purpose is making shareable social content; sharing the studio's own link shows a blank/unbranded preview, costing free distribution.
- [ ] **"Pipeline complete!" banner fires before the video actually finishes rendering.** The `complete` flag is set when the SSE `complete` event arrives (script/storyboard/captions ready), which happens *before* `renderVideo()` finishes stitching clips. A user can see "Ready to post" while the video panel still shows "Rendering clip 6/12."
- [ ] **No upfront wait-time framing.** First-time users get no expectation-setting (e.g., "this takes about 90 seconds") before starting generation — just a progress bar with no context.
